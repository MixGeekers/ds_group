1: # 001 Domain model
2: 
3: This document names the concepts of dsh-superagent and the rules each one keeps. The code, the tools the agents call, the panel and the other documents use these terms and no others. Following [000 Philosophy](000-philosophy.md), the model holds only concepts the work needs, and a business need becomes an attribute of one of them.
4: 
5: ## Flow
6: 
7: Work moves from Trigger to Eval to execution. A Source produces Triggers. A Trigger fans out as context to the Tickets subscribed to its Threads. A person's message that reached a Ticket this way stays with that Ticket; every other Trigger enters Eval, which decides whether it asks for new work. When Eval accepts, it creates a Ticket with a Mandate. An Assignment hands the Ticket to an Executor, and results go back to the Source through a Channel.
8: 
9: ```mermaid
10: flowchart LR
11:   S[Source] -->|Trigger| T[Thread]
12:   T -->|batch| E[Eval]
13:   T -->|Subscription fan-out| K[Ticket]
14:   E -->|accept with Mandate / stop| K
15:   E -->|ignore / unavailable| X[recorded]
16:   P[Policy change] -->|notice| K
17:   K -->|Assignment| W[Executor]
18:   K -->|Channel| S
19: ```
20: 
21: ## Concepts
22: 
23: Source, Thread, Eval, Mandate and Subscription form the core domain; the rest supports them.
24: 
25: | Concept | Definition | Rules it keeps | Why it exists |
26: | --- | --- | --- | --- |
27: | Source | A stream of events. A Source kind is a reusable implementation; a Source instance is one configuration of a kind: whether it runs and what it listens to | Holds its read position and its own bot identity. Defines what counts as an event, including conditions that only hold over time. Drops the bot's own messages and Triggers outside what it listens to before they enter the domain. Translates each Trigger into the Threads it belongs to and the Channel addresses to reply on | A restart must not replay events |
28: | Trigger | One immutable event with the Source's own metadata, such as its actor | Belongs to one or more Threads. Accepting a Trigger twice creates one Ticket | The unit of audit and of idempotence |
29: | Thread | Triggers in time order; the Source defines which Triggers form one Thread | At most one Eval runs on it at a time. It may have several Tickets. It holds its Subscriptions, the running Eval and its Decision history | Gives Eval a consistent view and its context |
30: | Correlation | The relation between Threads that one Trigger belongs to | Adds context; never routes | Lets Eval follow related evidence |
31: | Channel | The write side of a Thread; the Source defines its addresses | A Thread may have several Channels; a Ticket holds a set of them | Acknowledgements, replies, pull requests and resolved reviews are all writes |
32: | Policy | The versioned assessment rules and each Source's part: who may originate work through that Source, in that Source's own identities, how the list is synchronized, and which bots may make candidates | The Policy version covers the rules, the accept mode and the Source parts. A change that bears on a live Ticket, such as a change to a rule it was accepted under, is delivered to its Assignment; it does not revoke Mandates. Identities on different Sources are never mapped to each other | When the rules change, a running Eval must be discarded |
33: | Eval | The judgement on a batch of Triggers, in two layers: gate and assessment | Input: the Trigger batch, a Thread snapshot, every Ticket on the Thread including ended ones, and the current Policy. Output: a Decision. Holds no state | Decides whether new work starts, goes on or stops |
34: | Mandate | What an accept grants: the Policy version, the scope, the strength, the matched rules, the sender's identity on that Source and how the Source received the Trigger | Immutable; wider scope means a new Ticket. A Ticket's end does not end it: only a person's stop withdraws it, and a Policy that no longer holds it revokes it. Revalidated against the current Policy before an ended Ticket resumes | States why the work may be done, and how far it may go |
35: | Ticket | A unit of authorized work | Only Eval creates one. Refers to its Mandate; holds its Channels and Inputs. Goes open → ended, and back to open when a person's message on one of its Threads resumes it, unless a person stopped it. Defines its own state instead of following the Executor's | Owns the execution and its reports |
36: | Assignment | The binding of a Ticket to one Executor instance | A Ticket has several over time. Goes pending → running → ended independently of the Ticket. Only its final report may end the Ticket on the Executor's side | The Executor is outside the domain, but who executes must be inside it |
37: | Subscription | A many-to-many relation between Tickets and Threads, held by the Thread | A matching Trigger fans out to every live subscribed Ticket, with no check of who sent it; a person's message that reaches a Ticket this way takes no Eval. A Thread an Executor follows for context reaches its Ticket the same way, but lends it no authority and does not resume it. An ended Ticket keeps its Subscriptions, including that of a pull request that closed or merged: a person's message resumes it under the same Mandate, with no Eval or Decision; messages from bots and CI do not reach it, since its Executor reads its Threads again when it resumes. A Ticket a person stopped receives nothing. The main Session attaches a Thread to an existing Ticket only when its message points at that Ticket explicitly. A person can drop every Subscription on a Thread; an Executor can drop only its own Ticket's Subscription to a Thread it finds unrelated, except the Thread its Ticket started on and a Thread a Source made it follow, such as its pull request's | Later discussion reaches the Executor without anyone mentioning the agent again |
38: 
39: A person is not a domain concept. A Trigger carries its actor's identifier as the Source gives it. An allowed list is a Source's part of the Policy, in that Source's identities. That one person has accounts on several Sources is prompt text that the agents read when they need it.
40: 
41: The Executor, with its Sessions and directories, is outside the domain and connects through Assignments.
42: 
43: Aggregates: a Source holds its read position. A Thread holds its Subscriptions, its running Eval, which runs serially and merges Triggers that arrive meanwhile into the next batch, and its Decision history. A Ticket holds its Assignments, Channels and Inputs, and refers to Decisions by id.
44: 
45: ## Rejected concepts
46: 
47: | Rejected | Reason |
48: | --- | --- |
49: | Principal or Membership as an entity, or mapping identities across Sources | A person is Trigger metadata; an allowed list is a Source's part of the Policy; a cross-Source mapping is prompt text |
50: | Eval scheduling its own delayed Triggers | A condition that needs waiting is an ordinary Source, so Eval never depends back on Sources |
51: | Waiting or revoked Eval states | Waiting belongs to Sources; an Eval that runs out of time queues its Triggers again, or ends `unavailable` |
52: | At most one live Ticket per Thread | One Thread can carry several independent pieces of work; the assessment avoids duplicates from the Ticket list |
53: | An "append to Ticket" Decision | Appending is Subscription fan-out and needs no Eval |
54: | Admission checks on Subscriptions | A Subscription covers the whole Thread; the Mandate separates authorization from context |
55: | Correlation that routes | Subscriptions route; Correlation only adds context |
56: | Ticket state that follows the DSH Session | A Ticket can be handed off, resumed, or run by several Executors, so it defines its own state |
57: | An Executor declaring that a person asked it to stop | Only Eval decides the strength of an end |
58: | Cancelling running Tickets on a Policy change | A cancelled change leaves half-finished work; the change is recorded and delivered instead |
59: | Locking all of an Executor Session's tools when it fails or blocks | It would refuse a person working directly in the Session, and the report after recovery; pausing automatic input is enough |
60: | Inferring "waiting on a person" from a report's free text | The classification needs structured facts; the Executor asks through a structured field |
61: 
62: ## What is reusable
63: 
64: The concepts above and their rules are reusable. The Source implementations, the Policy contents, the Executor and the Channel implementations belong to a deployment. Each Source keeps its own words internally, such as message, chat or notification, and translates them into Triggers.
65: 
66: | Part | In this repository |
67: | --- | --- |
68: | Source | Feishu: live events, history recovery, polling of subscribed topics. GitHub: notifications, subscribed Issues and pull requests, CI changes, pull requests merged or closed, and, where switched on, every new Issue, pull request and comment in the repository and the default branch's CI |
69: | Policy | Assessment rules, and each Source's allowed list, its sync, its bot list and its admission table |
70: | Executor | A DSH Executor Session and a directory per Ticket, in an execution environment whose variables the Executor and the Sources register |
71: | Channel | Feishu topic replies, GitHub comments and review thread replies |

1: # 003 Execution
2: 
3: This document follows an accepted Ticket through execution, delivery and recovery. It builds on [001 Domain model](001-domain.md) and [002 Authorization](002-authorization.md).
4: 
5: ## Ticket stages
6: 
7: Each open Ticket has one stage, determined by what it is waiting for. Read the table from top to bottom; the first matching row wins.
8: 
9: | Stage | Condition | Panel label |
10: | --- | --- | --- |
11: | person / delivery | Delivering an Input to the Executor Session failed; it is retried after 2 seconds, then after a delay that doubles up to a minute, and a success, a new Assignment or the Ticket's end clears it | Input undelivered |
12: | person / answer | It has an Attention | Awaiting answer |
13: | person / review | The latest Report is `ready` | Awaiting review |
14: | person / blocked, failed | The latest Report is `blocked` or `failed`, and no person's message reached the Ticket since | Blocked · Failed |
15: | working / queued | No Assignment, or a pending one | Queued |
16: | idle | The latest Report is `waiting`, or the Assignment ended | Waiting for a Trigger |
17: | working | Everything else | Working |
18: 
19: A `person` stage means the Ticket cannot continue without human input. A new human message releases a `blocked` or `failed` Ticket to its Executor, so it moves to the next matching stage, usually `working`.
20: 
21: An `idle` Ticket has finished its turn without an outstanding question. A Trigger on a subscribed Thread can continue it.
22: 
23: ## The Executor
24: 
25: Each Ticket gets an Executor Session named `worker-<ticket id>` and a directory named after the Ticket's short name. Directories are created under the configured execution root, which defaults to `<stateDir>/work`.
26: 
27: Executor Sessions run commands without approval as the operator's OS user. Their `dsh-superagent-worker` preset contains the tools needed for work and moves foreground shell commands to background jobs after 5 seconds. This lets a worker receive new messages at the next step instead of waiting for a long command to finish.
28: 
29: The main Session uses `dsh-superagent-router`, with the same 5-second shell and job-wait limits and a smaller tool set for routing. Sessions retain the preset selected at creation. Worker subagents use DeepSeek Harness's `standard` preset.
30: 
31: ### Background work and worker slots
32: 
33: When a turn ends with shell jobs or subagents still running, the Ticket keeps its worker slot and stays working. It is not reported as waiting, and the main Session receives no waiting report. A completed job or subagent wakes the Executor with its result in the same slot. Once a turn ends with no background work, the Executor reports waiting normally.
34: 
35: After each turn, background work has at most 30 minutes to finish. At that limit, shell jobs are killed and the Ticket is reported waiting. The report includes the Executor's last words and which jobs were killed or subagents remain active. Subagents are allowed to continue. The Executor reads killed-job results on its next turn.
36: 
37: A handoff to a person frees the slot immediately and leaves the Report in effect. This applies after a `ready` Report or a question, unless another Input arrived since, and after any `blocked` or `failed` Report.
38: 
39: At handoff, subagents stop. Shell jobs, such as a server offered for review, may continue until the Assignment ends. They hold no slot and are exempt from the 30-minute limit, including after later turns. Their completion does not wake the Executor; it reads the results on its next turn.
40: 
41: A person can also start a turn by writing directly in the Executor Session. Its background work follows the same rules.
42: 
43: When an Assignment ends, its shell jobs are killed without waking the Session. Its subagents are released, and any completion turns they wake are dropped. The replacement Assignment and its waiting Inputs start after that shutdown finishes.
44: 
45: Stopping or restarting the plugin inside the DeepSeek Harness process uses the same shutdown path. Inputs not yet taken into Session history are retained. Shutdown waits up to 10 seconds for each worker's background work; a process restart ends that work outright. An Assignment interrupted while busy restarts with a recovery note.
46: 
47: Worker subagents, including nested ones, use DeepSeek Harness's `standard` preset with the worker's permissions. They retain only the worker's allowed work tools, and lose those tools when the Ticket ends. Under `standard`, a shell command moves to the background after 60 seconds. A subagent's jobs are killed when it ends.
48: 
49: ### Preparing a Ticket directory
50: 
51: Before the first Assignment, if the Ticket directory does not exist, the prepare command runs once with `sh` in the execution root. It has ten minutes to finish. Without a prepare command, the worker starts in an empty directory.
52: 
53: A failed command fails the Ticket and records its output. A retry or a person's message on one of the Ticket's Threads tries preparation again. A typical command uses `git worktree` to create a checkout. A pull request belongs to the Ticket when its branch's last path segment matches the Ticket's short name.
54: 
55: ### Concurrency
56: 
57: A Ticket holds a worker slot from the start of a launch, including preparation, or from the delivery that wakes its idle Executor. It also holds the slot throughout an active turn.
58: 
59: The configured concurrency limit applies to these slots, with one extra slot for a person's message or a status request. Such a wake can use the extra slot when normal slots are full, and takes the next free slot before a new launch. New work therefore cannot prevent a person from continuing an existing Ticket.
60: 
61: Other Inputs, such as CI results, wait for a normal slot. A launch waits in `working / queued` until a slot is free. Launches run concurrently, so a slow prepare command delays only its own Ticket.
62: 
63: ### Execution variables
64: 
65: The prepare command and Executor instructions receive the same execution variables. The command reads environment variables; the Executor reads their values and descriptions in its prompt. None contains credentials.
66: 
67: | Variable | Registered by | Value |
68: | --- | --- | --- |
69: | `DSH_SUPERAGENT_DIR`, `DSH_SUPERAGENT_NAME`, `DSH_SUPERAGENT_TICKET` | Executor | The Ticket's directory, short name and id |
70: | `DSH_SUPERAGENT_BLOCKED_COMMANDS` | Executor | Commands the Executor may not run |
71: | `DSH_SUPERAGENT_GITHUB_REPOSITORY` | GitHub | The repository the Source watches, `owner/name` |
72: | `DSH_SUPERAGENT_GITHUB_ACCOUNT`, `DSH_SUPERAGENT_GITHUB_COMMIT_EMAIL` | GitHub | The agent account and its commit email |
73: | `DSH_SUPERAGENT_GITHUB_NUMBER`, `DSH_SUPERAGENT_GITHUB_PR_HEAD` | GitHub | The Issue or pull request that started the Ticket, and its head ref when it is a pull request |
74: | `DSH_SUPERAGENT_FEISHU_BOT`, `DSH_SUPERAGENT_FEISHU_CHAT` | Feishu | The bot, and the chat that started the Ticket |
75: 
76: Ticket-specific values come from its directory and originating Trigger. Other values come from the current deployment settings. Only the panel can change execution environment settings; text sent to the main Session cannot change the configured shell command.
77: 
78: Workers reach GitHub through the agent account's `gh` and Feishu through the bot's `lark-cli`. Blocked commands fail when invoked by name.
79: 
80: These wrappers and directories establish defaults, not a security boundary. A worker can still execute a program by absolute path.

118: ## Failure and recovery
119: 
120: ### Executor blocked or failed
121: 
122: When an Executor Session reports `blocked` or `failed`, or fails to start or errors, which records `failed`, the Ticket takes no automatic Input.
123: 
124: A passing failure of the model provider or the network, an HTTP 408, 429 or 5xx or a refused, reset or timed-out connection, is tried again first, three times with a growing backoff: a launch runs again, and a worker whose turn failed is told to go on; a failed prepare command is not tried again.
125: 
126: Once failed, CI and bot messages are recorded on it, but they are not delivered and do not wake the Session.
127: 
128: Its tools stay available, so a person can continue in the Session directly.
129: 
130: A launch that failed is not tried again on its own.
131: 
132: A person's message, a later Report that is neither `blocked` nor `failed`, or a retry ends the hold.
133: 
134: It is worked out from the stored Reports and Inputs, so a restart keeps it.
135: ### Retry
136: 
137: Available while an open Ticket is in `person / delivery`, `person / blocked` or `person / failed`: a new Assignment, which starts its worker again and takes the Inputs that wait, is the remedy for each.
138: 
139: Once a person's message reached a blocked or failed Ticket, the Executor has the message, so the Ticket shows as working, or as queued when a failed launch starts again, and offers no retry, unless delivering that message failed.
140: 
141: A retry ends the current Assignment and starts a new one in the same Executor Session, directory, branch and pull request.
142: 
143: The Executor model applies only when a Session is created; an existing Session keeps its model.
144: ### Restart
145: 
146: Running Assignments end with "host restarted" and new ones start with a recovery note that tells the Executor to check its directory, git history and GitHub before repeating any outside action, oldest Ticket first.
147: 
148: Assignments that are waiting, ready, blocked or failed stay as they are, unless an Input reached them after that Report, in which case they start again too.
149: 
150: Sessions, directories, Subscriptions, pending messages and the stored work below survive a restart, and so does the record of which messages each Executor Session took into its history, which a step does once it claimed them and before its model call: a message taken by a turn the restart cut off counts as read once a Report or the end of a turn follows, while one a step claimed but a cancel or error cut off before the history counts as unread.
151: 
152: A retry or a restart gives the new Assignment again the messages delivered to the Assignment it replaces that its Executor Session did not take, which a cancel or a cut-off step may have dropped; a copy still waiting in the Session, kept by a restart or persisted across a crash, is taken out before the new Assignment's first message, so the new delivery is the only copy.

44: ### Engine
45: 
46: `Engine` (`src/core/engine.ts`) is the only writer of Triggers, Threads, Decisions, Tickets and Assignments. It receives these ports from `src/core/ports.ts`: `Repositories`, a current-Policy getter, `SourceScope`, `Assessor`, `Executor` and `Outbox`. Optional dependencies are `prepareTicket`, `now` and `emit`.
47: 
48: All Engine methods are synchronous. Each command changes its aggregates in one transaction. `evaluateDue` uses a separate transaction for each Eval or Ticket it changes. Ports enqueue external work inside the transaction; adapters perform it after commit. Invalid requests throw `UserError`.
49: 
50: The Engine exposes one entry point and delegates to five internal parts. It owns each command's transaction. Commands spanning parts, such as `attach`, `evaluateDue` and `policyChanged`, call the parts in sequence.
51: 
52: Each part extends `EnginePart` (`src/core/part.ts`), which provides options and shared reads. Dependencies between the parts flow in one direction:
53: 
54: | Part | Responsibility | Depends on |
55: | --- | --- | --- |
56: | `Intake` | A Source's Trigger: stored once with its disposition, recorded on its Threads and their correlations, fanned out to the subscribed Tickets and, unless it is a person's message that reached one, queued for Eval on its primary Thread | Tickets |
57: | `Evals` | The Evals of Threads: batching, the gate's strength, actor and basis with the Trigger that gave them (`primary`), the deadline from the candidate's delivery and its shift by a pause, the requeue of an expired Eval, candidates, replays and hand-backs, the requeue on a Policy change, and the Decision each ends with: accept, stop, ignore or unavailable | Tickets |
58: | `Tickets` | The Ticket lifecycle: creation from an accept with its Mandate, Subscriptions and first Assignment, the Threads it follows and their closes (`followsDone`), attached Threads and Threads followed for context, Inputs and notes, resume and the messages a resume refuses, retry, restart, stop and end, the Subscriptions it drops, and the notice of a Policy change to the Tickets it bears on | Replies |
59: | `Assignments` | What the Executor and its workers report: an Assignment started, Inputs reached it or failed to, a restart interrupted it, and a worker's Report with its question for a person | Tickets, Replies |
60: | `Replies` | Channel writes through the `Outbox`: the acknowledgement, the answer, the result and the attention message, each by the rule that allows it | Nothing |
61: 
62: Commands use imperative names such as `stopTicket`; facts reported by adapters use past-tense names such as `assignmentStarted`. The query `candidate` uses a noun.
63: 
64: Callers receive only their role's `Pick` of the Engine: `EngineForSources`, `EngineForMainSession`, `EngineForPeople`, `EngineForSettings` or `EngineForExecutor`.

490: ### Main Session tools
491: 
492: The main Session has 14 application tools:
493: 
494: | Purpose | Tools |
495: | --- | --- |
496: | Read and route | `orchestrator_read`, `orchestrator_route` |
497: | Manage Tickets | `orchestrator_ticket_retry`, `orchestrator_ticket_request_status`, `orchestrator_ticket_note` |
498: | Re-evaluate | `orchestrator_trigger_requeue` |
499: | Assess proactive work | `orchestrator_eval`, `orchestrator_eval_read`, `orchestrator_eval_decide` |
500: | Communicate and unsubscribe | `orchestrator_reply`, `orchestrator_thread_unsubscribe` |
501: | Configure | `orchestrator_settings_save`, `orchestrator_policy_sync`, `orchestrator_prompts_save` |
502: 
503: `orchestrator_route` accepts 1–20 decisions in order. Each accept, attach, stop or ignore runs as a separate Engine command. It also permits ignoring an inferred candidate directly from its card. `orchestrator_ticket_note` sends a fact as context, not authority.
504: 
505: Settings saves select `policy`, `eval`, `executor`, `pause`, or `source`; the last also names its Source id. A prompt save with `preview: true` composes text without saving.
506: 
507: The main Session also has DeepSeek Harness's `skill`, `bash`, `read`, `read_image`, `job_output`, `job_list` and `job_kill`. Avoiding code reads is a prompt rule, not a tool restriction.
508: 
509: ### Presets and wait limits
510: 
511: New main Sessions use `dsh-superagent-router` (`ROUTER_PRESET`). Shell commands and job waits return after 5 seconds. If the Web UI selects another preset before the first turn, `Sessions` restores this preset, or `WORKER_PRESET` for a worker.
512: 
513: The `noWait` guard in `src/app/sessions.ts` refuses main-Session shell commands that invoke `sleep` at a command position, shell `timeoutMs` above 30 seconds, and `job_output` waits above 5 seconds. Reports and job results arrive automatically, so waiting only delays incoming candidates.
514: 
515: Existing main Sessions on `standard` also need explicit timeouts for foreground commands and job waits; otherwise that preset's defaults hold the step for 60 and 30 seconds respectively. The host determines the current preset through `presetForSession`, falls back to the Session projection or header, and assumes `standard` if it cannot read the value.
